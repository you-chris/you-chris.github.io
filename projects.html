Build-A-Being Yourself

A web-based conversational AI platform for healthcare research

Build-A-Being Yourself (BABY) lets domain experts rapidly deploy embodied conversational interventions at scale, with no programming required. It coordinates agentic workflows across dialogue planning, strategy selection, and LLM-driven response generation. The platform has been used across NIH-funded studies in mental health, cancer clinical trial enrollment, and patient simulation for university curricula.

Built with Node.js, React, Express, AWS, OpenAI, LLMs, RAG, Python, MongoDB, REST APIs

buildabeing.com

2021 – presentAI agentsAI/MLDigital health

ALEX: Clinical trial enrollment

NIH R24 project using virtual humans to widen clinical trial participation

This NIH-funded project uses virtual human technology to increase clinical trial participation among older adults from minority communities (Black/African American, Hispanic/Latin, and rural adults over 50). We designed the virtual human ALEX to be culturally sensitive, tracked engagement in NIH-funded trials, and compared ALEX with standard recruitment methods. Clinical trials are queried and summarized in real time with LLM prompting and a retrieval-augmented chatbot.

Built with HTML, JavaScript, CSS, Unreal Engine, MetaHumans, machine learning models, Python, ChatGPT

Papers

  • The impact of health literacy in a virtual agent intervention addressing barriers to cancer clinical trial participation 2025
  • Exploring the influence of multiple virtual agents for addressing barriers to cancer clinical trial participation 2025
  • Real-time virtual human for promoting clinical trial education and recruitment 2024
  • Applying positive-unlabeled learning for fraudulent data detection in online clinical trial recruitment In preparation

AI agents in mental health conversations

Generative-AI virtual humans for motivational interviewing

Asynchronous online mental health platforms are accessible and flexible, but they often lack the connection that keeps people engaged. This project uses generative-AI virtual humans to hold more natural, adaptive motivational interviews, and studies how they affect users' readiness for change, acceptance of mental health interventions, and trust in the therapeutic process.

Built with Node, JavaScript, CSS, AWS, OpenAI, three.js, machine learning models, Python, FastAPI

Papers

  • Alter egos alter engagement: Perspective-taking can improve disclosure quantity and depth to AI chatbots in promoting mental wellbeing 2025
  • Social perspective-taking and human-AI collaboration can promote change talk and readiness in agent-delivered motivational interviews In review

Classifying motivational interviews

Fine-tuned BERT and data augmentation to classify change, sustain, and neutral talk

Embodiment and agency of virtual avatars

Challenges in evaluating agency and embodiment in virtual reality

Participants controlled avatars with increasing degrees of movement (head only, head and torso, full body). More control increased embodiment, but current measures of the sense of agency proved inconsistent, pointing to better ways of measuring how control and agency interact in VR.

Built with Unity, Python

Papers

  • A sense of urgency on the sense of agency: Challenges in evaluating agency and embodiment in virtual reality 2024
  • What my bias meant for my embodiment: An investigation on virtual embodiment in desktop-based virtual reality 2024

2019 – 2022Virtual reality

Redirected walking: strafing gain

Redirecting users one diagonal step at a time

Redirected walking lets people explore virtual spaces larger than their physical room. Strafing gain adds small lateral movements so the user walks a diagonal path while keeping their original orientation. In a study with 27 participants, users could be redirected along a 5.57° diagonal to the right and 4.68° to the left without noticing; a follow-up with 10 participants confirmed orientation was maintained.

Built with Unity, C#

Papers

  • Strafing gain: Redirecting users one diagonal step at a time 2022
  • Strafing gain: A novel redirected walking technique 2019
